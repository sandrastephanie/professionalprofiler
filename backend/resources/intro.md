## the short version
i'm a product manager in copenhagen, and the shortest way to describe me is curious and a bit restless (in the good way). i like making complicated things feel simple, and i burn for tech because of the autonomy it gives me: if i have an idea, i can build it myself instead of waiting for someone else to.

## the traveller
i've travelled solo to more than a dozen countries, and i track every one of them in an app (watching the map fill in is weirdly satisfying). solo travel is my favourite kind of system reboot. i follow my own curiosity, talk to people i normally wouldn't and end up learning as much about myself as about the place. it's the rawest form of anthropology, which is also how i like to work: watch what people actually do.

## the ambitious one
i'm ambitious, and i don't hide it. every job i've left, i've left because someone offered me something more exciting. my career hasn't been a straight line up a ladder - i've grown outward instead of upward, from copywriting for banks, to running marketing in fintech, to product. i call it closing the fintech circle: payments, accounts payable, EDI, debt collection, and now building the product itself.

## tech and autonomy
what i love most about tech is the freedom. with tools like Claude Code i went from writing tickets to making PRs myself, and i've built three websites on my own just because i wanted to. i get genuinely excited when PMs build things (a PM that vibe codes? GIRL YES).

## the people part
i care loudly about women in tech. i mentor women who are growing their careers in male-dominated industries, and i'm about to start teaching a webinar series on Claude Code for women in tech, because tech literacy is quickly becoming the new divide and i want more women on the right side of it.

## the honest bit
my brain is wired for optimisation, and i'm notoriously bad at switching off. (i still play the sims, and even there i have to max out every career before i get bored and start a new save.)
