## making the complicated simple
most of my work comes down to the same thing: taking complex, manual financial workflows and turning them into simple, scalable product flows. how do we remove unnecessary work and help people take the right action at the right moment?

## research both ways
i combine qualitative research (interviews, observation, talking to customers) with quantitative data (GA4, Hotjar, conversion and funnel analysis). i care about what people actually do, not what we assume they do. i often use research to find the edge cases in complex industries and turn them into pragmatic improvements.

## the slightly annoying questions
i'm the one asking where this data comes from, what happens if the integration fails, and what the business actually needs. i'd rather ask early than find out in production.

## data over gut feeling
i don't make decisions on gut feel. data and real customer insight first.

## hands-on
i'm not the type to throw requirements over the fence. i'm comfortable at roadmap level and in the details of one interaction, workflow or backlog item. i prototype with AI tools, i've made PRs myself, and i work closely with engineers instead of around them.

## commercial and product at once
with a marketing background, i keep the commercial case in view: pricing, positioning, what sales hears from customers, what it costs to operate. i bridge business value, customer needs and the tech team.

## agile or structured, depending on the problem
i pick the approach that fits - agile when we need to learn fast, more structured when the domain is regulated and the cost of mistakes is high.

## direct
my most common feedback is that i'm "too direct". i say the thing, kindly, and i'd rather have the honest conversation early.
